HOMANWX WEATHER SAFETY GUIDE

Understand the Risks

Learn what severe-weather outlooks mean, why every risk matters, and what to do when storms threaten.

IMPORTANTAn outlook is not a warning. HomanWX is an independent forecasting project, not an official NWS/SPC product. Always check weather.gov for official watches and warnings.

A low risk is not zero risk

Even one isolated severe storm can cause serious injury or damage. A lower category generally describes the expected coverage and intensity of severe weather across a region—not how dangerous a particular storm will be at your location.

01 / CATEGORICAL OUTLOOK

What do the five severe-weather risk levels mean?

TSTM indicates general thunderstorms and is not one of the five numbered severe-risk categories. These are broad outlook categories, not tornado intensity ratings.

03 / MAP SYMBOLS

What does significant-severe hatching mean?

Diagonal hatching identifies a specifically designated area where significant severe weather is forecast. It does not mean every location in that area will experience a significant event.

For SPC-style outlooks, significant thresholds are generally EF2+ tornadoes, hail at least 2 inches, or thunderstorm wind gusts at least 75 mph, depending on the hazard. The exact hatching convention and probability threshold depend on the outlook product.

HomanWX hatching is forecaster-designated. Always interpret it alongside the forecast discussion and official SPC guidance.

04 / TAKE ACTION

Outlook vs. watch vs. warning

Outlook · Be aware

Severe weather may develop during the forecast period. Review your plan, charge devices, and follow updates.

Watch · Be ready

Conditions favor severe weather. Know your safe place and be prepared to act quickly if a warning is issued.

Warning · Take action

Dangerous weather is occurring or imminent. Follow the warning instructions immediately. For a tornado warning, move to a basement or small interior room on the lowest floor, away from windows.
